Milky Way Unrolled · Dublin

The whole Milky Way laid flat. Its plane runs across the middle, with the centre of the galaxy in the middle of the picture and longitude increasing to the left. Your horizon is the wave: as the Earth turns, it rolls across the band.

There are three ways to flatten the sky. Flat spreads it evenly by longitude and latitude (the plate carrée projection), so it stretches the sky more and more towards the top and bottom. Mollweide and Hammer (Hammer–Aitoff) fold it into an oval that keeps every patch of sky at its true size: Mollweide keeps the lines of latitude straight, and Hammer bends them but keeps shapes truer near the edges.

  • Your horizon, with its compass points. The sky below it is dimmed
  • Hatched: the part of the sky that never rises from Dublin
  • Overhead, with dotted rings at 30° and 60° up
  • The ecliptic: the Sun's path, near which the Moon and planets run
  • Sgr A*, the black hole at the centre of the galaxy, 26,700 light-years away
  • Messier globular cluster
  • Messier open cluster
  • Messier nebula (bright, planetary or supernova remnant)
  • Messier galaxy: they keep away from the band, where dust hides what lies beyond
